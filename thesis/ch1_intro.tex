\section{Background and Motivation}
Daily portfolio allocation is a sequential decision-making problem in which asset weights must be continuously adjusted as market prices and conditions change. An effective strategy must balance return generation, financial risk, and transaction costs. However, relying only on current-day market indicators may overlook important temporal patterns, while maximising raw returns may not adequately account for risk. Frequent portfolio rebalancing can also increase transaction costs and reduce net returns.

This study investigates three complementary mechanisms within a common Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) framework: temporal memory through a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) encoder, risk-aware reward shaping using the Differential Sharpe Ratio (DSR), and turnover control through a quadratic weight-change penalty. Their individual contributions are examined through four controlled models (M1--M4) using chronological walk-forward evaluation on ten NSE equities, with a passive Equal-Weight portfolio as the benchmark.
